# Touch riding controls

The left half is a floating steering/driving pad. Push forward to accelerate; pull back to brake, then keep holding back to reverse once nearly stopped. Switching from reverse to forward also brakes first. Once all four wheels are airborne, the left thumb also controls native fore/aft lean. The vertical center stays at the original touch-down point across takeoff: a pullback already held on the ramp immediately supplies backward lean when all wheels leave the ground. Takeoff throttle and gear stay held until landing or touch release. The gesture-generated ground brake clears in air because pullback now means lean; independent controller/keyboard brakes remain available. Ground braking resumes when wheel contact returns. This corrects the previous takeoff recentering, which discarded an already-held pullback. Horizontal input continues to the original cross-up moment and lateral air force. This is limited original midair correction, not an added flip or spin impulse. The browser direction adapter uses a one-foot/second standstill tolerance; this is a browser convenience, not a recovered original controller mapping.

Anywhere on the right half of the gameplay canvas is a hold/lean/release surface when pressed on the ground. Press and hold to prepare preload, drag up or down to lean forward/back while holding, and release near the ramp lip. There are no separate preload, brake or reverse buttons. A small marker appears at the touch origin and shows lean movement; it says **RELEASE** only when Rust reports native preload arming, and briefly **PRELOADED** when the native force is active. **PRELOAD** feedback appears on every grounded press, even when a ramp is not yet eligible; native availability turns its border blue. There is no accumulating charge bar. The small Pause and Camera HUD buttons remain separate hit areas.

## Native preload and the touch adaptation

The exact USA v2.00 build has a recovered lean-flick measurement at `001eb0d0` and rider-force routine at `001f0ae0`. Existing instruction comparisons in the private `drivetrain-rider-oracle.json` cover 700 grounded and 500 airborne cases with no mismatched compared fields. This is component instruction evidence, not a hardware or full-game match.

The original routine records time while lean exceeds 0.8. Crossing below −0.7 emits the axis rate. The force routine arms while grounded, moving forward above 30 feet/second and rising above 5 feet/second; lean back refreshes a 0.25-second arming window. A sufficiently fast forward flick starts native preload force for up to 0.25 seconds. Holding longer does not accumulate extra power, and pressing on flat ground does not grant a free jump.

The requested touch design separates physical lean from preload preparation. An optional `preload_axis` channel supplies +1 during right hold and −1 for one submitted simulation-input batch on release, while the ordinary lean axis follows vertical dragging. Left airborne lean and right physical drag add and clamp to the original −1…+1 range; a neutral right hold leaves left pitch unchanged, while opposite deliberate drags cancel. This lets the rider adjust pitch while holding without accidentally releasing the preload. Only the original arming/flick checks read the optional channel; wheelie, body lean and air control retain the physical lean input. Native force magnitude, timing and terrain gates are unchanged. When this optional channel is absent, keyboard and controller input use the original shared lean axis. This input adaptation is authored browser behavior, not a newly discovered original controller scheme.

Release is consumed at the simulation boundary so a visual-frame timer cannot expire while the worker is busy. Unseen taps, pointer cancellation, focus loss, resize, reset and menu transitions do not synthesize release. A second right finger cannot steal the held gesture. Releasing the right finger leaves left-hand throttle/steering active.

## Airborne stunts

A new press on the right half while airborne becomes a swipe surface: up selects Nac Nac, down No Footed Can Can, left Suicide No Hander, right Bar Hop. Hold the finger after swiping to extend the original stunt; release lets the main motion finish. These four gestures map to the original Circle-plus-direction family. They do not alter ATV velocity or apply an animation to the vehicle. A grounded preload hold never changes into a stunt just because the wheels leave the ground.

The runtime loads all 24 original main stunt MOTs and all 24 original held extensions from the rider MCF catalog. Controller Circle, Triangle and L1 combinations plus stick direction address all 24; keyboard J, K and L supply the three modifiers with arrow directions. Touch currently exposes the four Circle-family stunts. Controller left-stick Y now uses the correct original sign (up leans forward), and R3 changes camera so Triangle is free for its original stunt modifier. Trigger drive/brake remains available alongside bottom/left face-button drive/brake; bottom plus left face buttons select reverse.

Recovered routines `002d9530` and `002d9870` determine modifier rearming, direction thresholds and held extension. Entry `002da6c0` requires no wheel contact, horizontal world speed above 4 feet/second, and an expired reposition timer; the exact 2-second reset timer is preserved. Per-stunt minimum main-motion times come from exact-build control tuning. The main clock pauses during extension; releasing resumes it. The selector and evaluator `002d8ba0/002d8e50` use the original main/hold motions and snapshot fade. Looping hold motion is sampled using the recovered one-dimensional gradient noise (`003454c0`, initialized by `00345650`), **not a sine wave**. A local original-RNG seed is used, so whole-game shared RNG order is not claimed.

The original manual's printed pages 7–8 describe the jumping-only down/up preload gesture, airborne cross-up and all 24 button/direction stunts. Printed page 11 describes blue preload opportunity and orange achieved preload. The recovered native HUD update `002e2290` agrees: orange records achieved strength and fades after the event, rather than filling while a button is held. Source: [original USA instruction manual scan](https://www.videogamemanual.com/PS2/ATV%20Offroad%20Fury%202%20%28USA%29.pdf).

## Presentation and remaining controls

Gameplay surfaces and their HUD suppress mobile text selection, selection gestures and the touch callout. This applies only to the game document and its stylesheet; the separate development log remains selectable. The touch marker disappears in menus and on focus loss. Non-error diagnostic copy is hidden during touch driving; errors stay visible.

Normal rider and named main/hold stunt animations are connected. Original scoring, profile restrictions, combo landing bookkeeping, crash transitions and rider IK remain incomplete. The port currently exposes the recovered basic entry gates in its free riding state; it does not pretend the wider race/profile flags exist. Native `001e8690` crashes on any wheel contact while holding a stunt, or while a released main clip has at least 0.5 seconds remaining (`002d8a20`). `Animator::stunt_landing_safe()` exposes that exact predicate for crash integration. The score system's separate 0.5-second grounded grace is not an animation auto-finish rule. Crash state still needs to cancel/settle the stunt through its original transition. The workbench's older generic swipe display remains separate from these actual driving controls. Profile preload adjustments and whole-game replay equivalence also remain incomplete.

## Verification

`node --test web/touch.test.mjs` checks seven state scenarios, including airborne pitch with preserved takeoff drive state and four directional stunt swipes. Its grounded scenarios cover independent lean/preload, exactly-once release, cancellation/reset/unseen taps, pointer ownership, and braking before both forward/reverse direction changes. Native rider tests verify the original ramp gates, reject flat/slow/airborne launches, and demonstrate that physical forward/back lean changes while the optional preload channel is held do not fire the force; releasing that channel does.

`tools/test_touch.mjs` is an opt-in actual-content browser check. It uses actual Chromium touches at several points across the right half, observes submitted WASM inputs, checks continuous lean during hold, release and cancellation, simultaneous left/right operation, braking and automatic reverse/forward transitions, focus/menu pause, narrow layout and game-only selection suppression.

`tools/test_touch_ramp.mjs` drove straight from the original Caribou Peak start using simultaneous touches, held the right half, and released when native arming appeared. The revised gesture produced native active preload, strength 1 and airborne wheel contacts. One captured run measured upward speed of 9.28 feet/second at arming and 25.02 feet/second during active preload. The inspected screenshot shows the airborne quad and transient marker. This is an observed trajectory, not a paired experiment isolating preload from slope and other forces. No vehicle state or forces were injected. The trace and screenshot are ignored artifacts under `work/verification/touch-anywhere-ramp.{json,png}`.

Chromium checks do not establish physical iOS Safari or Bluetooth-controller compatibility. Those hardware checks remain outstanding.

The latest Caribou browser check also moved the left thumb after takeoff and observed native airborne lean/steer input, retained throttle, changed orientation and the negative pitch-rate sign implied by original ATV01 `AeroMomentScaleX=-2`. `tools/test_touch_stunts.mjs` drove from the real start, triggered original Nac Nac while airborne, observed a changing original rider pose, reached the native 0.92-second hold threshold, and verified that release resumes the main clip. Rear wheel contact had returned by that run's held-extension sample, so it is not evidence of an airborne held pose. No body state or force was injected.

A private actual-content animation probe sampled all 24 main clips and 24 held clips through start, hold, release and completion: 8,640 finite original rider poses. This checks the MOT runtime and catalog integration, not complete original stunt scoring or crash equivalence. New core tests cover all 24 modifier/direction selections, native start thresholds, reset cooldown, modifier rearming, held-clock pause/resume and noise initialization/periodicity.

A private 600-case comparison executes original `002d8ba0/002d8e50` instructions for modes 4 and 5 and compares Rust main/hold clocks, snapshot transitions, blend weight and clamped sampling phase: zero mismatches. Pose sampling and noise return are hooked, and arithmetic uses finite host binary32; this is not PS2 hardware or full scoring/input equivalence.

## Takeoff continuity and retained-page recovery

The takeoff recenter was removed after the user reported ineffective pullback. In the revised actual Caribou test, pullback began at step 52, grounded brake input appeared at steps 55/58/61, and the first observed airborne frame at step 70 already carried backward lean +1 with the touch-generated brake cleared. Native pitch rate subsequently reached −0.998 radians/second; body tilt reached 30.38 degrees during the short observed gesture. The test does not modify forces or pose and does not establish a complete backflip. A separate normal preload run returned to four wheel contacts after touch release and drove again, verifying landing followthrough. Prolonged full pullback can overturn the ATV; complete original crash/reposition behavior remains separate work. Captures: ignored `work/verification/touch-held-pullback-ramp.{json,png}` and `touch-baseline-landing.json`.

`tools/test_lifecycle.mjs` covers a verified browser lifecycle defect: the old unconditional `pagehide` handler terminated the Rust worker and closed audio even when the browser retained the page. Persisted hides now suspend rendering/audio and clear input while retaining resources; persisted shows restart one render loop with fresh timing. The frontend separately resumes menu polling. Actual-content WebKit and Chromium checks drove airborne, dispatched **synthetic** persisted hide/show events, verified suspension then continued body motion with the same worker, checked stale throttle clearing, and exercised pause/resume. Natural Back navigation in the tested installations reloaded the page instead of using BFCache. Thus this regression proves the event-handling fix, not that retained navigation caused the user's hovering report. Ordinary host and public touch driving/release/resume attempts did not reproduce persistent hovering.

`tools/test_touch_pose.mjs` separately verifies the restored pose-only preload feedback: stationary right hold with neutral physical lean and release produce different original rider matrices, while native preload force remains inactive. The inspected `work/verification/touch-preload-pose-held.png` and `touch-preload-pose-released.png` show that pose change. This is a visual preparation cue, not evidence of a free jump or accumulated charge.
